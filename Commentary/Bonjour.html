[03.11.26] Check Your Ego

Bonjour Tristesse by Françoise Sagan

From the Back:

Endearing, self-absorbed, seventeen-year-old Cécile is the very essence of untroubled amorality. Freed from the stifling constraints of boarding school, she joins her father-a handsome, still-youn widower with a wandering eye- for a carefree two-month summer vaction in a beautiful villa outside of Paris with his latest mistress, Elsa. Cécile cherishes the free-spirited moments she and her father share, while plotting her own sexual adventures with a "tall and almost beautiful" law student. But the arrival of her late mother's best friend, Anne, intrudes upon a young girl's pleasures. And when a relationship begins to develop between the adults, Cécile and her lover set in motion a plan to keep them apart... with tragic, unexpected consequences.

The internationally beloved story of a precocious teenager's attempts to understand and control the world around her, Françoise Sagan's Bonjour Tristesse is a beautifully composed, wonderfully ambiguous celebration of sexual libration, at once sympathetic and powerfully unsparing.

Bonjour Tristesse was like watching a slow motion train crash in one of the more enjoyable ways and the crash wasn’t that bad. Still a causality though, but you’re like “oh that could have been a lot worse”. Maybe the lesson with this one is cynicism (+ ego) will usurp your joy, always. It might give you that little brain tingle because “you’re right” when bad shit happens, yet dare I say, we attune ourselves to what we want to see - what we believe is reality. Reading made me mildly nervous, but it is so short (113 pgs), easy to read and the chapters are like 4 – 5 pages long. That’s kind of all I have to say about that. For Sagan being 18 when she wrote this, I think its very well written and perceptive. I don’t think I was as articulate of my own thoughts or impressions at 18. I don’t remember.

My two cents on cynicism/nihilism: sure you opt to view the world in this down turned way (defaulting to the worse, letting the void consume you), so you resign yourself to external systems and move in the flow with that. Now you’re still unhappy AND exist in a condition you “can’t fix” (everything is a choice including doing nothing). You might as well do what you want to do that brings you internal nourishment, because why not if everything is so fucked? Cynicism is not your crutch. It is your handicap.

I am fully blaming Cécile’s 40 year old father and windower, Raymond, for her cynical disposition towards love. He loses my sympathies because I don’t like the type of man, a player, that puts women in tricky, competitive situations against each other because he can’t decide if he wants to listen to his brain, heart or dick. On a vacay in the Mediterranean at a rented white cottage, Raymond invites Elsa, his dumb, young, beautiful, mistress at the time to join he and Cécile. It’s just them for a few weeks and then he gets the percolation he’ll invite Anne too, his dead wife’s friend whom they still have a 15 year familial relationship with, because he is interested in her in a long term way. Just take a second and visualize this scenario…

I think that’s inappropriate. Not just for Cécile, but also Anne. Your teen daughter is watching you [a man in which she has a healthy parental relationship with and of the gender she is attracted to in kind of in an oedipus way] triangulate women. What type of influence are you? She observes him through their interactions continually. Him admiring Elsa and Anne when they are next to each other. Him ignoring Elsa and tuning into only Anne. Elsa finally picking up on his attraction to Anne and doing things to make her jealous with Raymond. That’s messy. I am not surprised in Cécile’s cyncial disposition about love because she has an example of a way in which a man can move with women: the satisfaction of the externals only: the eyes, ears, and body. Cécile can understand these secular satisfactions because she is sexually active, but she has never experienced love. He isn’t helping her set an example so much so that when he does display love to Anne, Cécile feels in conflict with it.

I like Anne enough. She’s Raymonds age, sophisticated, intelligent, withholding of drastic reactions, perceptive and the complete opposite of Raymond’s flings. I like how she would call Cécile out on the dot when she would start mouthing off, especially about love, and then just show disdain. It’s important to note she wasn’t fully cold to Cécile. She just didn’t give their interactions more energy than required. She was regularly sweet and motherly to Cécile and when Cécile did something she didn’t like she told her and moved on. Though Anne was irked by Elsa’s presence, she always maintained her composure. Her simply being her was the temptation. Anne does what she wants.

At a point in the story where the crew go to a casino (cuz Elsa wanted to go to the casino), Raymond completely ditches Elsa with his daughter to go sit in his car with Anne and talk sweet nothings. Cécile drove Anne’s car and Raymond tells Cécile to tell Elsa that he and Anne went home because she was sick (a lie). Anne is beside herself at Cécile telling him he was being rude and gently persuading her to listen to her father. I was disappointed with Anne here. Not only is this teenager shit, but it is my slef-respect truly, that in times I have been attempted to be triangulated by a man, I don’t give in ever. I don’t play games. I don’t play jealous. I don’t play favorite. You want to do this this and that with another woman, go ahead. You’re not involving me. This was the one time I approved of Cécile’s mouth flying off the handle and she got slapped for it by Anne! {add quoute “”}. Yea, I bet you didn’t want to hear that. I was so frustrated with this scene because there was no adult in this situation. None of the adults (aka, most importantly, her father) were doing what they were supposed to be doing and she got reprimanded. Have some shame.

The introspection that caused Cécile to concoct her evil plan was a shrug. The apple don’t fall far from the tree. Wonder where she got that manipulative itch from. Besides it wasn’t really about Anne coming in and shaking up her and Raymond’s bohemian lifestyle with “structure”. It mostly kicked off because Anne, assuming position as mother, forbade her from seeing her summer fling, Cyril. This was for two reasons: 1) she has to focus on her studies (which she hates doing) and 2) it will “end her up in the hospital” (aka an abortion). I’m like, let the girl study during the weekday, tell her about contraceptives and let her see her little boo on the weekends. All work and no play makes Jack a dull boy and teens are going to do what they want to do. It’s also like, so y’all get to have this love and expectant marriage, that Cécile is continually a witness to whether she wants to be or not, and she can’t do anything? Hello??

Back to the plan, I was annoyed in the way in which Cécile sets it in motion and then is fearful of it. She could just make it stop, but she doesn’t. This ego of feeling smart because she can read and manipulate people. This ego making it hard for her to come clean. The pride doesn’t let her stop the plan because ego usurps all joy behind false masks of surface level achievement and makes it painful to admit you did wrong. Top that with cynicism, this disposition that everyone is self-interested and doing things for their own end [which auto-opts greed as inherit to human nature], and now you have a wombo-combo for investing energy into malefic things that effects everyone negatively just for a little, temporarily scratched itch. Awesome.

Cécile gets Elsa (who loves her father) and Cyril (who loves her) to parade around as lovers to make her father jealous in ways he might do something regrettable behind Anne’s back. She convinces them to be apart of the plan by lying that Anne is making her and her father’s life terrible. In reality, they are both very happy with Anne around. The devil, in this case Cécile, can hang bait right in your face all day, but if you don’t take it, then nothing happens. It takes two to tango. Raymonds dick did the thinking and he took it. Dumbass. What comes around goes around, truly. Want to play in people’s faces? Okay, get played by your daughter that results in an irreversible consequence. Good job, guys.

After the whole matter, Cécile and her father just go on like normal I guess. It’s written in that sutble French way where things are physically sounds and turmoil is quiet. They go back to Paris, have a funeral and carry on their bohemian ways, with careful consideration of what happened that summer. I guess best outcome is that Cécile and Raymond are in some capacity able to reflect in on their actions. Not enough for them to adjust their lifestyle, but a considerate amount to have some delicacy towards things. Idk.

If there’s anything I’ve learned from this book and 90 day fiancee it is: nip it (whatever the issue is // “drama”) in the bud asap, if it can be helped. Slow down, take a breath and say what your ego doesn’t want you to say (an apology, admittance of fault, silence/a held tongue to listen, believing and being okay with hearing something you don't want to hear). Solid 3/5. Turns out there’s a movie for the book and I’d maybe watch it. Gives the same energy as those “summer I turned pretty” type of movies. Not really my style, but I’ll watch anything, more or less.
